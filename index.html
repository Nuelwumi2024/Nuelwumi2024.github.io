Power BIRetail profitability

Why is profit flat while sales boom?

Atlas Global Retail (fictional) case study

Challenge

Sales grew 43% in four years, but profit grew only 6%. The COO wanted to know where the money was going before setting the 2027 budget.

Approach

  • Modelled 9,818 order lines, customers, products, returns and targets as a star schema
  • Wrote DAX for margin, budget vs actual, and discount and loss analysis
  • Built a four-page report, from overview to root cause

Results

  • Sales within 1% of target, but profit 16% short ($488.8K vs $579.3K)
  • Orders discounted 30%+ sold $505K and lost $105K
  • Average discount crept from 10.8% to 14.5%
  • A 20% discount cap would add about $125K profit (+26%), volume held constant

Power BIPeople analytics

Who is leaving, why, and what does it cost?

HR attrition case study for a fictional retailer

Challenge

Leadership saw rising recruitment costs but had no view of where attrition was concentrated or why people were leaving.

Approach

  • Star-schema model of 1,500 employee records with active and inactive date relationships
  • DAX for attrition rate, tenure and age bands, early attrition and replacement cost
  • Three-page report: overview, reasons for leaving, drivers

Results

  • 27.5% attrition, an estimated $13M replacement cost
  • Customer Support lost 49% of staff; 61% of leavers had under three years' tenure
  • Overtime lifted attrition from 23% to 38%
  • Five costed recommendations for leadership

Why do wallet customers go quiet?

Churn and retention data story for a digital wallet

Challenge

A wallet's Growth team needed to know why customers stop transacting and where to spend acquisition budget.

Approach

  • Analysed 5,000 customers across 2024 signup cohorts in SQL and Python
  • Built a cohort heatmap, compared channels and tested early activity against retention
  • Wrote ranked recommendations with a correlation-versus-causation caveat

Results

  • Retention falls 78% → 60% → 39% at months 1, 3 and 6
  • Organic customers retain 1.4x better than paid social
  • 6+ transactions in 14 days makes customers 1.9x likelier to stay active
  • The top 10% of customers drive 42% of volume

Where does fraud concentrate?

Transaction fraud analysis and rule recommendation

Challenge

A payments team needed to know where fraud concentrates and which low-friction rules catch the most of it for the least analyst workload.

Approach

  • Analysed 117K transactions in SQL, computing fraud lift against the base rate
  • Evaluated rules on precision, recall and review rate
  • Wrote a recommendation with a quantified trade-off

Results

  • 664 fraud cases: 0.57% of transactions but 2.07% of value
  • Fraud is about 3x likelier from midnight to 5am and on accounts under 14 days old
  • Four simple rules catch 29% of fraud while reviewing 7.7% of traffic
  • Rules miss about 71%, which makes the case for a model next
